# Ordinary card-art review

## Previous presentation
- 48 shared 336 × 224 cartoon-style images, keyed by circle and type, illustrated a catalog of 226,400 records.
- Per-record hue rotation could change important faction colors without changing the actual composition.
- Frames used broad pastel washes and glossy-looking UI accents; the art had a different visual vocabulary from the requested new direction.
- The fitted duel stylesheet hid every illustration on screens shorter than 700px.

## Changes
- Replaced all 48 artwork files: 40 newly generated narrative compositions, plus eight explicitly documented chalk-ink adaptations after the daily generation limit was reached.
- Subjects now follow foundation-card themes: errands, supernatural household objects, civic buildings, envoys and communal undertakings.
- Authored circle palettes replace arbitrary hue shifts; a versioned image URL avoids stale browser images.
- Added matte parchment card frames, restrained pigment accents, serif headings, printed-looking cost badges and ornamental indigo backs. Scope is Ordinary cards only.
- Kept illustration panels visible in fitted matches, including shorter screens. Existing full-rules inspection, selection, exhaustion and attack indicators are retained.
- Preserved every artwork path, catalog record, card ID, mechanic, deck rule and saved-deck format. The group-printing algorithm and engine were not changed.
- Both the main page and demo load the same new stylesheet; the service worker and downloadable archive include the artwork.

This is an artwork/presentation review, not a fresh balance audit of the entire procedural catalog. See ART.md for exact coverage and reuse.

## Additive bank continuation

Review found that each expansion faction/type still repeated just one artwork slot despite the new print direction. The additive continuation retains all 48 slots and every original catalog record, adds 150 paper-toned field-guide adaptations from existing project-generated source art, and routes expansion cards by fixed ID. Grouping now includes the resolved image as well as the original grouping key, exposing different visual printings without merging previously separate groups. Curated starters, card frames, selection, combat and deck serialization remain unchanged. The daily image-generation limit prevented fresh paintings; the bank manifest explicitly records the reused sources and adaptations.
